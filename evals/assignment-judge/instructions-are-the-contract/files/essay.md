# Role and status

A social status is a position a person holds in a social structure, such as teacher or daughter. A social role is the set of behaviours expected of whoever holds that status. Status is the position; role is what the position asks of you. For example, being a student is a status. Attending class, studying for exams and handing in work on time are parts of the student role. Roles can conflict when one person holds several statuses, for example a student who is also a parent and must choose between studying and caring for a child. Statuses can be ascribed, like age, or achieved, like a degree; each one carries its own role, and the expectations of a role are learned through socialisation in the family, at school and at work.
