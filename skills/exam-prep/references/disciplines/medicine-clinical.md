# Medicine and clinical professions

## Degrees covered

- Medicine (רפואה, MD), Dentistry (רפואת שיניים, DMD), Veterinary Medicine (רפואה וטרינרית).
- Nursing (סיעוד, B.S.N.), Midwifery, Paramedic studies (רפואת חירום).
- Physiotherapy (פיזיותרפיה), Occupational Therapy (ריפוי בעיסוק), Speech-Language Pathology and Audiology (קלינאות תקשורת), Nutrition and Dietetics (תזונה), Optometry, Medical Laboratory Sciences, Pharmacy (רוקחות), Public Health (בריאות הציבור), Health Systems Management.
- Pre-clinical science content (anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pharmacology) is shared with life sciences; this pack covers the clinical reasoning, documentation and professional-writing side.

## Typical deliverables

- Case studies / case presentations (הצגת מקרה): history, examination, differential, work-up, management, follow-up; oral and written.
- Care plans (nursing, OT, PT): assessment → diagnosis → goals → interventions → evaluation, in the profession's framework (nursing process, ICF model, OTPF).
- SOAP notes and clinical documentation exercises; prescription-writing exercises; drug calculations.
- Evidence-based practice (EBP) assignments: a PICO question, a search strategy, a critical appraisal of 2–5 papers, a recommendation.
- Reflective writing (reflection on a clinical encounter), professionalism and ethics essays.
- OSCEs and practical exams; multiple-choice exams with clinical vignettes; anatomy spot tests.
- Public-health: epidemiological calculations (incidence, prevalence, relative risk, NNT), program proposals, policy briefs.

## Answer conventions

- Clinical reasoning is written as a chain: findings → problem list → differential diagnosis ranked by likelihood and danger → investigations that discriminate → management with rationale. "Red flags" are named explicitly and first.
- Differential diagnoses are justified per item: what supports it, what argues against it, what would confirm or exclude it.
- Documentation follows the format exactly (SOAP: Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan; nursing process: ADPIE; ICF: body functions/structures, activities, participation, environmental and personal factors). Headings are not optional.
- Numbers come with units and reference ranges (Na⁺ 128 mmol/L [135–145]); drug doses as dose/kg where relevant, with the calculation shown; times as 24-hour.
- Evidence is graded: level of evidence or GRADE rating stated when the course teaches it; "studies show" is never enough.
- Patient privacy: cases are de-identified (initials or "a 54-year-old woman"); no real names, ID numbers or dates of birth, even in a classroom assignment.
- Goals in care plans are SMART and patient-centred; interventions have a rationale tied to the assessment.
- Reflective writing uses a model (Gibbs, Kolb) if the course names one, and distinguishes description from analysis from action.
- Hebrew documentation keeps standard clinical abbreviations and drug names in English; the course may require generic names only.

## What graders look for

- Safety first: red flags identified, dangerous diagnoses not missed, contraindications checked.
- A ranked differential with explicit reasoning for and against each item.
- Investigations chosen to change management, each with a stated purpose.
- Format discipline: the profession's documentation structure followed exactly.
- Evidence cited from guidelines or primary studies with the level of evidence; recommendations consistent with that evidence.
- Patient-centredness: goals phrased from the patient's perspective, cultural and language factors noted.
- Calculations shown and double-checked (dose, infusion rate, NNT, RR).
- Reflection that analyses and plans change, not a diary entry.
- Professional writing: de-identified, respectful, no judgmental language about patients.

## Common mistakes

- Jumping to one diagnosis and fitting the findings to it (premature closure); omitting the dangerous alternatives.
- A list of tests with no rationale; ordering everything.
- Mixing subjective and objective findings in the wrong SOAP section; putting the plan into the assessment.
- Dose calculations without units, or mg/kg used with the wrong weight; decimal errors (10× errors are the classic).
- "Studies show" or citing UpToDate as if it were primary evidence when the assignment asks for primary studies.
- Nursing diagnoses written as medical diagnoses, or goals that are interventions in disguise.
- Reflections that describe at length and analyse in one sentence.
- Identifiable patient details in an assignment.
- Hebrew texts that translate drug or disease names inconsistently; use the course's convention throughout.

## Default citation style

Vancouver (numeric, ICMJE) in medicine, dentistry and pharmacy; AMA in some medical programmes; APA 7 in nursing, OT, PT, speech-language pathology, nutrition and public health. Guidelines cited with organisation, title, year and URL/DOI. The course syllabus always overrides this default.

## Worked breakdown

Invented assignment: "Case study — community-dwelling patient after a hip fracture" in a second-year nursing course. Deliverables: a written care plan (max 8 pages) in the nursing-process format, a one-page evidence summary with 3 sources, due in 2 weeks; 25% of the grade; individual.

| Part | Type | Weight | Depends on | Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Assessment: organise the case data by functional health patterns, flag abnormal findings with reference ranges | analysis | 20 | — | 2.5 h |
| 2. Nursing diagnoses: three prioritised (ABC + safety first), each with "related to" and "as evidenced by" | analysis | 20 | 1 | 2 h |
| 3. Goals and interventions: SMART goals, interventions with rationale, including fall prevention and pain | design | 25 | 2 | 3 h |
| 4. Evidence summary: PICO question on one intervention, 3 appraised sources, level of evidence | analysis | 20 | 3 | 3 h |
| 5. Evaluation criteria and documentation format | other | 15 | 3 | 1.5 h |

Suggested order: assessment table first — the diagnoses come out of what is abnormal; then diagnoses; then goals/interventions; search the evidence for the intervention you are least sure about; finish with evaluation criteria. Environment: Word with the course's care-plan template, Zotero (APA 7), CINAHL/PubMed for the three sources, CASP checklist for appraisal. Review first: the lecture on the nursing process and the falls-prevention guideline the course uses. Constraints to check: whether a NANDA-I diagnosis list is mandated, the page limit, and the de-identification rule for the case.
