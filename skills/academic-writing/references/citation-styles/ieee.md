# IEEE

Numeric, in order of first citation. Engineering, computer science, electrical engineering.

## In-text and list rules

- In text: [1], [2], [3]–[5] or [1, 3]; the number goes inside the sentence punctuation: "as shown in [2]."
- The same source keeps its number throughout.
- Reference list ordered by number, not alphabetically; initials before surname; up to six authors, then "et al.".
- Hebrew sources: keep the Hebrew title, add a bracketed English translation when the course asks: [in Hebrew].

## Examples

1. [1] J. Smith and K. Lee, "Title of article," *Journal Name*, vol. 12, no. 3, pp. 45–67, 2020, doi: 10.1000/xyz.
2. [2] A. Author0 et al., *Big Book*. City: Pub, 2015.
3. [3] R. Katz, "A conference paper," in *Proc. Conf. Name*, City, 2019, pp. 1–8.
4. [4] "A page." Site Name. 2021. [Online]. Available: https://example.org/p
5. [5] J. Smith, "A thesis," Ph.D. dissertation, Some University, 2017.
6. [6] א' כהן, *שם הספר*. הוצאה, 2019.

## Checks

- Every in-text citation ⇄ list entry: `scripts/check_citations.py`.
- Entries with fields from the student's own source only; an entry that cannot be resolved (DOI, catalogue, database) is kept and marked `[unverified]`, never completed from memory.
