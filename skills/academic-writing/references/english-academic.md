# Academic English, and what Hebrew speakers get wrong

Register: formal but plain. Prefer the precise verb to the noun phrase ("we measured" over "a measurement
was performed"), one claim per sentence, hedging that matches the evidence (*suggests*, *indicates*,
*is consistent with* — not *proves*). First person is accepted in most fields today ("we argue"); check
the course. Spelling: one variety (US or UK) throughout.

## Paragraphs and argument

- Topic sentence that makes a claim, evidence, analysis, a link to the next paragraph.
- Signposting without padding: *First*, *In contrast*, *This suggests*; avoid *In this paper I will talk about*.
- Quotations short and always followed by your own analysis.

## Errors Hebrew speakers make

Each item: the pattern, an example as written, the fix. In `edit` mode these are corrected and listed in the
change list; in `feedback` mode the three most frequent are named with one example each.

1. **Missing article** (Hebrew has no indefinite article): "We conducted experiment" → "We conducted an experiment".
2. **Article on general plurals/abstract nouns** (from ה־): "The society is changing" (in general) → "Society is changing".
3. **Wrong preposition from Hebrew ב/ל/על**: "depends in", "interested on", "discuss about" → "depends on", "interested in", "discuss".
4. **Preposition with "married/meet/enter"**: "married with", "entered to the room" → "married to", "entered the room".
5. **Present simple for ongoing/finished actions** (Hebrew tense system): "Since 2019 I study this" → "Since 2019 I have studied this".
6. **Tense consistency in methods**: mixing "we measure" and "we measured" → past tense for what was done.
7. **Word order of adverbs**: "We checked carefully the data" → "We carefully checked the data".
8. **Word order in questions/indirect questions**: "It is unclear what is the cause" → "It is unclear what the cause is".
9. **Adjective after the noun**: "a method new" (rare) and stacked noun calques "the system of the payment" → "the payment system".
10. **"Make/do" and other light verbs**: "make a research", "do a decision" → "conduct research", "make a decision".
11. **Countable "information/research/knowledge"**: "researches", "an information" → "studies", "information".
12. **Overuse of "very" and "a lot"**: "very important", "a lot of researchers" → "crucial", "many researchers".
13. **"According to me", "In my opinion" as openers** (calque of לדעתי) → state the claim with its evidence.
14. **Comma splices** (Hebrew joins clauses with commas): "The data were noisy, we filtered them" → "…noisy, so we filtered them" or a semicolon.
15. **False friends and calques**: "actual" for *current* (אקטואלי), "sympathetic" for *nice*, "until now" for *so far* in the wrong sense, "pass a course" vs "take a course".

## What `edit` keeps

The author's voice, terminology, argument and structure. Language only: grammar, register, concision,
consistency. Every change goes into the change list; meaning changes are proposed, never made silently.
