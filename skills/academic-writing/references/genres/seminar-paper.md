# Seminar paper (עבודה סמינריונית)

A 15–30 page argument on a research question within a seminar's theme, built on scholarly sources; in Israeli universities usually the main written requirement of a seminar and the step before a thesis.

## Structure

- Title page (course, lecturer, student, date) and table of contents
- Introduction: topic, research question, why it matters, the thesis (the answer you will argue), roadmap
- Literature review / theoretical framework: the debate you enter, organised by position, not by author
- Body chapters: each advances one step of the argument; claim → evidence → analysis → link back to the question
- Discussion: what the argument shows, counter-arguments and limits
- Conclusion: the answer restated, contribution, open questions
- Bibliography in the course's citation style

## What examiners reward

- A research question narrow enough to answer in the page limit
- A thesis visible on page 1 and traceable through every chapter
- Engagement with the debate (who disagrees, and why you side as you do)
- Primary sources or data analysed, not only summarised
- Accurate, consistent citation; no unsupported claims

## Common mistakes

1. A topic instead of a question ("Bureaucracy in Israel" vs. "Why did the 2009 reform not shorten waiting times?")
2. Literature review as a list of summaries, one per author
3. Chapters that describe without arguing; the thesis disappears after the introduction
4. Quotations left to speak for themselves, with no analysis after them
5. Conclusion that introduces new material or only repeats the introduction

## Hebrew and English

Write in the submission language. Hebrew papers keep English terms in parentheses at first mention and follow `references/hebrew-academic.md`; English papers by Hebrew speakers get a pass with `references/english-academic.md`.
