# Lab report (IMRaD)

The standard report of an experiment in the sciences, engineering and health: Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion, with an abstract written last.

## Structure

- Title: the variables and the system ("Effect of X on Y in Z")
- Abstract (150–250 words): aim, method, key result with numbers, conclusion
- Introduction: background, gap, aim, hypothesis
- Methods: materials, procedure, analysis plan; reproducible, past tense, passive or "we"
- Results: figures and tables first, prose that points at them; no interpretation
- Discussion: interpretation, comparison with expected/literature values, error analysis, limitations
- References; appendices with raw data and calculations

## What examiners reward

- A testable hypothesis stated before the results
- Methods detailed enough to repeat
- Figures with labelled axes, units, error bars and numbered captions
- Results separated from interpretation
- Quantitative error analysis, not "human error"

## Common mistakes

1. Interpreting data in the Results section
2. Missing units or uncertainty in tables
3. Methods copied from the handout in the imperative
4. "The experiment proved…" instead of "supports"
5. Discussion that ignores results contrary to the hypothesis

## Hebrew and English

Write in the submission language. Hebrew papers keep English terms in parentheses at first mention and follow `references/hebrew-academic.md`; English papers by Hebrew speakers get a pass with `references/english-academic.md`.
