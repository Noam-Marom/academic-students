# Arts and performance

## Degrees covered

- Visual arts: painting (ציור), sculpture (פיסול), photography, printmaking, new media and video art.
- Performing arts: theatre (תיאטרון), acting and directing, dance (מחול), music performance and composition (מוזיקה).
- Film and television (קולנוע וטלוויזיה), screenwriting, animation.
- Art history and theory taught within art schools (the historical side is also covered by humanities).

## Typical deliverables

- Studio work: a body of work, an exhibition, a performance, a film, a composition, presented to a jury or audience.
- Process documentation: sketchbooks, rehearsal journals, production diaries.
- Artist statement (הצהרת אמן) and project proposal.
- Critical analysis of a work: a painting, a performance, a film scene, a score.
- Screenplays, treatments, storyboards, shot lists.
- Theory courses: essays and presentations.

## Answer conventions

- The creative work itself belongs to the student. Help is in planning, research, critique, structure, documentation and writing about the work — never in producing the work that is graded as the student's own artistic output.
- Analysis of a work moves from description (what is there: materials, composition, movement, sound) to analysis (how it works) to interpretation (what it means, in context) to evaluation.
- Use the discipline's vocabulary precisely (composition, chiaroscuro, blocking, mise-en-scène, phrase, motif, montage).
- Artist statements are short (150–400 words), first person, concrete about materials and process, free of jargon, connected to influences without name-dropping.
- Proposals state the idea, the form, the process, the resources, the timeline and the risks.
- Process journals are honest and reflective: what was tried, what failed, what changed, why.

## What graders look for

- A coherent body of work with a clear artistic intention.
- Development over time, visible in process documentation.
- Technical skill appropriate to the year.
- Ability to talk and write about the work critically and in context.
- Risk-taking and responsiveness to critique.
- In analysis papers: close observation and well-supported interpretation.

## Common mistakes

- Artist statements full of abstract theory and no description of the actual work.
- Analysis that only describes, or jumps to interpretation without evidence in the work.
- Process journals written after the fact.
- Proposals without a realistic timeline or budget.
- Relying on a generated image, text or score where the course expects the student's own creation — this is usually treated as an integrity issue in arts programmes.

## Default citation style

Chicago (notes or author-date) for written work; MLA in some theatre and film departments. Works of art are cited with artist, title (italic), year, medium, dimensions and collection; films with director, title and year; performances with company, venue and date. The course syllabus always overrides this default.

## Worked breakdown

Invented assignment: "Solo choreography — 4 minutes on the theme of distance" in a second-year dance course. Deliverables: the performance at the end of semester, a rehearsal journal, a 300-word artist statement; 10 weeks; 50% of the course grade.

| Part | Type | Weight | Depends on | Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Research: three works on the theme, movement improvisations | analysis | 10 | — | 6 h |
| 2. Movement material: phrases, motifs, development | practical | 35 | 1 | 25 h |
| 3. Structure, music or sound, space and lighting choices | design | 20 | 2 | 10 h |
| 4. Rehearsal journal: weekly entries, feedback and changes | essay | 15 | 2, 3 | 5 h |
| 5. Artist statement | essay | 10 | 2, 3 | 2 h |
| 6. Performance | practical | 10 | 2–3 | — |

What the plugin can help with here: researching references, structuring the rehearsal plan, giving feedback on the journal and the statement's clarity, and planning the timeline. The choreography itself is the student's. Review first: the course's composition lectures and the feedback from the mid-semester showing. Constraints to check: time limit, music rights, technical rehearsal schedule.
