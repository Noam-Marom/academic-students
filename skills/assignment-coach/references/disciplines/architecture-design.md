# Architecture and design

## Degrees covered

- Architecture (אדריכלות, B.Arch.), Landscape Architecture (אדריכלות נוף), Interior Design (עיצוב פנים).
- Urban Planning (תכנון ערים), Industrial Design (עיצוב תעשייתי), Visual Communication (תקשורת חזותית), Fashion and Jewellery Design, Ceramics and Glass Design.

## Typical deliverables

- Studio projects (סטודיו): a semester-long design responding to a brief, presented at crits (ביקורות) and a final jury.
- Drawings: site analysis, plans, sections, elevations, axonometrics, details at specified scales.
- Physical and digital models; renderings; diagrams explaining concept and process.
- Portfolios (פורטפוליו) of selected work with short texts.
- Theory and history courses: essays and precedent studies.
- Technical courses: structural, environmental and building-systems calculations; construction details.

## Answer conventions

- A design is argued, not just shown: brief → site and user analysis → concept → development (iterations) → resolved proposal → how it answers the brief.
- Drawings follow conventions: scale bar and north arrow, line weights for cut vs projection, consistent titles, standard symbols, dimensions where required.
- Diagrams carry the concept; a presentation board has a hierarchy (one hero image, supporting drawings, minimal text).
- Precedent studies analyse why a building or object works (organisation, structure, light, materials), not only what it looks like.
- Process is part of the grade: sketches and iterations are kept and shown.
- Text on boards is short and in the studio's language; project names can be in English.

## What graders look for

- A strong concept that is legible in the drawings and diagrams.
- Response to the site, programme and users in the brief.
- Iteration: evidence of testing alternatives and learning from crits.
- Technical credibility: structure, circulation, accessibility, environment, regulations at the level the year requires.
- Drawing quality and conventions; clear board hierarchy.
- Verbal presentation: concise, confident, answers questions.

## Common mistakes

- Beautiful renderings with an unresolved plan.
- A concept that disappears between the diagram and the building.
- Ignoring the site (sun, access, topography, context).
- Inconsistent scales, missing scale bars, sections that do not match plans.
- Overcrowded boards with too much text.
- Leaving production to the last night: plotting and model-making always take longer.

## Default citation style

Chicago (notes or author-date) for theory and history papers; images credited with architect/designer, project, year and photographer or source. The course syllabus always overrides this default.

## Worked breakdown

Invented assignment: "Studio 2 — a neighbourhood library on a corner site" in a second-year architecture studio. Deliverables: site analysis board, concept model, plans/sections 1:100, two perspectives, final jury presentation; 14 weeks; 60% of the studio grade.

| Part | Type | Weight | Depends on | Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Site analysis: access, sun path, context, users | analysis | 15 | — | 12 h |
| 2. Programme and precedents: three libraries analysed | analysis | 10 | — | 8 h |
| 3. Concept: diagrams and massing models, three alternatives | design | 20 | 1, 2 | 20 h |
| 4. Development: plans, sections, structure, circulation | design | 30 | 3 | 40 h |
| 5. Final presentation: boards, model, renders, talk | practical | 25 | 4 | 30 h |

Suggested order: site and precedents in weeks 1–3; alternatives by the first crit; commit to one concept by mid-term; develop drawings and structure; freeze design two weeks before the jury and spend the rest on production. Environment: Rhino or Revit for the model, AutoCAD for drawings, InDesign for boards; physical models in the workshop. Review first: the brief, the site visit notes and the studio tutor's comments from each crit. Constraints to check: required drawing list and scales, board size, and whether renders are allowed or hand drawing is required.
