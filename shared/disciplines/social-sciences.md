# Social sciences

## Degrees covered

- Psychology (פסיכולוגיה), Behavioural Sciences (מדעי ההתנהגות), Cognitive Science.
- Sociology and Anthropology (סוציולוגיה ואנתרופולוגיה), Political Science (מדע המדינה), International Relations, Public Policy (מדיניות ציבורית), Government.
- Communication and Media Studies (תקשורת), Journalism.
- Social Work (עבודה סוציאלית), Criminology (קרימינולוגיה), Education (חינוך), Gender Studies, Geography and Human Environment (the human side), Middle Eastern Studies (the social-science side).

## Typical deliverables

- Seminar papers (עבודה סמינריונית): 20–40 pages, a research question, literature review, method, findings/analysis, discussion; the signature deliverable of the degree.
- Short response papers and reading summaries (סיכומי קריאה) on weekly readings.
- Empirical reports in psychology (APA format): a replication or small study with participants, measures, statistics.
- Statistics/methods assignments: SPSS or R output interpreted in words; research design critiques.
- Fieldwork reports (observations, interviews) in anthropology, sociology and social work; reflective journals in social-work practicum.
- Policy papers and briefs in political science and public policy; op-eds in communication.
- Exams: essay questions comparing theories or applying them to a case; multiple choice in large intro courses.

## Answer conventions

- A paper argues a thesis in answer to a research question; every section serves that argument. The introduction states the question, why it matters and the structure; the conclusion answers the question and names limitations.
- Theories are attributed: who said it, in which work, in which year, and what the key concept means in that author's terms before it is applied.
- Empirical claims cite empirical sources; normative claims are marked as such. Quotations are short and analysed, never left to speak for themselves.
- Psychology reports follow APA 7 exactly: title page, abstract, Introduction, Method (participants, materials, procedure), Results (statistics in APA notation: t(58) = 2.14, p = .036, d = 0.55, 95% CI), Discussion, References; figures and tables in APA format.
- Qualitative analysis shows its method (thematic analysis, grounded theory, discourse analysis) and its evidence (coded excerpts), and reflects on the researcher's position.
- Policy papers: problem definition, criteria, options, evaluation of each against the criteria, recommendation, implementation.
- Hebrew academic register: formal, no first person unless the course allows it, terms of art in Hebrew with the English in parentheses on first use (הון תרבותי (cultural capital)).
- Ethics: participants anonymised; informed consent mentioned when data were collected; vulnerable populations handled per the course's ethics guidance.

## What graders look for

- A clear research question and a thesis that answers it; a structure that follows the argument.
- Correct attribution of theories and precise use of concepts; no "society thinks".
- Engagement with the course readings specifically, not generic sources from the web.
- Method matched to question; limitations acknowledged honestly.
- Statistics reported fully and interpreted in plain language; effect sizes, not only p-values.
- Qualitative work with visible coding and reflexivity.
- Citation discipline: every claim traceable; reference list complete and in the required style; Hebrew and foreign sources formatted per the style's rules.
- Academic register and coherent paragraphs with topic sentences.

## Common mistakes

- Describing theories at length and applying them in one paragraph; or applying a concept without defining it.
- Summarising sources one after another instead of synthesising them around themes.
- Over-quoting; quoting Wikipedia or news sites for theoretical claims.
- Generalising from a small convenience sample; confusing correlation with causation; p-hacking by testing everything.
- APA details wrong: italics, running head, in-text author-year punctuation, "et al." rules, missing DOIs.
- Mixing Hebrew and English reference lists without the style's rules (APA: alphabetical by author within language; the course decides whether to separate).
- Missing ethics statement for interview or survey data.
- A conclusion that introduces new material or restates the introduction.

## Default citation style

APA 7 (author-year) across psychology, education, social work, communication and most sociology departments; Chicago author-date or footnotes in some political-science and anthropology courses. Hebrew sources follow the Hebrew adaptation of APA used by the department (author surname, first initial with geresh: כהן, א'). The course syllabus always overrides this default.

## Worked breakdown

Invented assignment: "Seminar paper — Social media use and political participation among students" in a third-year sociology seminar. Deliverables: a 25-page paper (APA 7), a 10-minute presentation, a one-page proposal due after 3 weeks; final due at semester end; 60% of the grade; individual.

| Part | Type | Weight | Depends on | Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Proposal: research question, 5 key sources, planned method | essay | 10 | — | 6 h |
| 2. Literature review: 15–20 sources synthesised by theme (participation theory, media effects, Israeli context) | essay | 25 | 1 | 20 h |
| 3. Method: online survey (n ≈ 100) or 8 interviews; instrument; ethics | design | 15 | 1 | 8 h |
| 4. Findings: descriptive statistics + one inferential test, or thematic analysis with excerpts | analysis | 25 | 3 | 12 h |
| 5. Discussion and conclusion: back to the theory, limitations, implications | essay | 15 | 2, 4 | 6 h |
| 6. Presentation | other | 10 | 1–5 | 4 h |

Suggested order: proposal with a narrow question (the most common failure is a question too broad for 25 pages); literature review written in themes from the start; method and ethics before any data; analysis; discussion; presentation last. Environment: Zotero from day one, Google Forms or Qualtrics for the survey, JASP for the statistics (or Taguette for interviews), Word with the APA 7 template. Review first: the course's methods lectures and the department's seminar-paper guide. Constraints to check: whether the department separates Hebrew and English reference lists, the page limit's definition (with or without references), and the ethics form the department requires for student data collection.
